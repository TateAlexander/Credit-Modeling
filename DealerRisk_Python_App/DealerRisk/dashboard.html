Model contract

A transparent, default-loss analytics MVP. Every result depends on the supplied inputs.

  • Fixed horizon: 12 months from the as-of date. PD is the probability of default in that horizon. EAD is the assumed exposure at default; LGD is the net loss fraction after recoveries and costs.
  • One row per borrower. Aggregate multiple loans to the same obligor first; provide a common borrower PD and exposure-weighted LGD. Rows are independent conditional on the model factors.
  • PDs, LGDs, asset correlation and gamma factors are supplied assumptions. The application does not estimate or validate these parameters from a loan snapshot.
  • The models hold EAD and LGD fixed within each scenario. Amortization, prepayment, recovery timing, interest income, and uncertain recoveries are not simulated.
  • Use non-defaulted loans. Already defaulted accounts need a separate recovery assessment. Default definitions must be consistent with your PD inputs.

Vasicek · finite portfolio

One shared normal economic factor drives correlated borrower defaults. Unlike the asymptotic formula, this simulation retains individual exposures and borrower-specific risk.

Z, εᵢ ~ independent N(0,1) Aᵢ = √ρ Z + √(1−ρ) εᵢ Dᵢ = 1[Aᵢ < Φ⁻¹(PDᵢ)] L = Σ EADᵢ × LGDᵢ × Dᵢ

ρ is latent asset correlation, not the observed default-indicator correlation. This is the credit portfolio model, rather than the Vasicek short-rate process.

CreditRisk+ · Monte Carlo implementation

Independent, mean-one gamma macro and sector factors mix conditional Poisson arrival intensities.

G₀, Gₛ ~ Gamma(shape = 1 / CV², scale = CV²) λᵢ = PDᵢ × [(1−w) + w(m G₀ + (1−m) G_sector(i))] Nᵢ | G ~ Poisson(λᵢ) L = Σ EADᵢ × LGDᵢ × Nᵢ

CV=0 uses deterministic factors equal to one. Factor weights sum to one. This implements the compound-Poisson/gamma loss approximation through simulation; it does not implement the analytical generating-function recursion.

Nᵢ can exceed one. PD inputs act as expected default-count intensities in this approximation; the probability of at least one count is not exactly the supplied PD. Losses can exceed the physical maximum. This approximation is most appropriate at low PDs and can be weak for high-risk BHPH portfolios. Counts are deliberately not capped, so the model assumptions remain explicit.

Reading the outputs

  • Expected loss: Σ PD × LGD × EAD. Exact for the implemented models; Monte Carlo means fluctuate around it.
  • VaR: the empirical loss quantile at your selected confidence level. It is a modeled loss threshold, not a confidence interval for estimated expected loss.
  • Expected shortfall: average loss in the worst (1−confidence) fraction of simulations, with fractional mass at a discrete quantile.
  • Capital buffer: max(VaR − expected loss, 0). An analytical buffer measure, not a regulatory or accounting capital requirement.
  • Monte Carlo SE: numerical standard error of the simulated mean. It does not include model or parameter uncertainty.
  • Stress: PD′ = min(PD × multiplier, 1); LGD′ = min(LGD + increase, 1). Dependence parameters remain unchanged. Base and stress reuse model-specific seeds.
  • Vintages: current delinquency snapshots by origination quarter. Different loan ages make these rates unsuitable as like-for-like lifetime default comparisons.
  • Concentration: expected-loss contribution, not VaR or ES allocation. A high contribution does not establish that collections intervention will help.

Tail simulations are especially sensitive to model choice and sample size. This MVP has numerical tests, but has not been calibrated or backtested on dealership performance data.
